# Structures for business and sales artifacts

Use these as decisions about reader needs, not mandatory page recipes. Preserve the user's governing structure and scale length to content, channel, and buying stage.

## Sales one-pager

Make category, audience, relevant problem, mechanism, and next action understandable without a presenter. Use one main commercial argument and a specific product/workflow example. Place proof beside the assertion it supports. Keep printed contact information usable; use a confirmed destination rather than inventing a salesperson's address.

If proof is absent, show an accurately labeled mechanism or illustrative workflow. Do not fill a proof slot with invented adoption numbers or quotes. A one-pager means one page unless front-and-back is requested. Simplify copy and grid before shrinking essential type.

## Company or service brochure

Assign distinct jobs to opening, content, and closing: explain what the company does, show whom it serves and how the offering works, demonstrate credible fit, and give a next step. An introduction needs more category context; a post-meeting brochure can reflect known needs. Choose page count from substance, not a fixed six-page formula.

Avoid adding company history, long biographies, market statistics, or a separate cover unless they earn space. Keep technical diligence and commercial terms appropriately detailed for the stage. Page roles may include overview, mechanism, applications, evidence, and contact rather than repeating the same cards throughout.

## Product catalog

Plan overview, individual product, comparison, application evidence, selection, and contact pages as appropriate. Show specifications that affect selection. Compare like units and distinguish capacity limits from normal operating values.

For large catalogs, maintain a working manifest: page, product identifier, source photo, approved copy, specification source, revision status. Keep photos paired with the right model. Preserve real shapes and components; generated applications must not masquerade as the supplied product. Give products enough printed size for inspection instead of forcing unlike forms into equal-size thumbnails.

## Proposal

Lead with the reader's challenge, proposed work, and intended outcome. Make scope, deliverables, responsibilities, dependencies, timetable, and price understandable where known. Distinguish assumptions from agreed commitments. Tailor to actual discovery, not merely the prospect's name.

Use authorized commercial terms. Never invent a fee, discount, guarantee, renewal clause, implementation commitment, or legal entity. If essential values are missing, prepare a clearly identified draft with explicit missing fields or ask for what is needed to finalize it. Do not conceal uncertain terms in polished prose.

## Case study

Explain customer context, baseline, intervention, measurement period, and attributable result. Retain denominators, units, dates, and method. Separate observations from interpretation. Use approved attributed quotes and accurate anonymous descriptions. With no real outcomes, deliver an outline or explicitly illustrative example, never a fabricated customer story.

## Pitch deck and demo companion

Speaker slides should support one useful point with details in notes. A forwarded deck needs enough explanation to stand alone. Use the slide count the content needs rather than prescribing ten or twelve.

Show a recognizable problem, mechanism, relevant fit and evidence, implementation realities, and next step. Prices, customer logos, ROI, and timelines depend on sources. Demo scenes should map to known buyer questions and leave room for interaction within the requested duration.

## Report, assessment, or executive brief

Make the conclusion or decision easy to find, then support it with evidence and analysis. Distinguish findings, interpretation, recommendations, and uncertainty. Use tables for comparable records, alternatives, or values; narrative findings often work better as prose.

Include methodology, benchmarks, recommendations, appendices, and contents when useful. Benchmarks need comparable populations, periods, and sources. Do not force every report to contain KPI cards, a dark cover, quick wins, or a universal recommendation matrix.

## Objection guide, persona card, and sales playbook

An objection guide records the concern, truthful response, evidence, known limitation, and follow-up question. Explain genuine uncertainty rather than overpromising a rebuttal.

Distinguish interview-backed persona knowledge from hypotheses about roles, goals, criteria, and influence. A playbook should be searchable by stage, audience, and scenario, with source owners/dates for changing facts. Do not presume a buying process or stereotype a role without evidence.
