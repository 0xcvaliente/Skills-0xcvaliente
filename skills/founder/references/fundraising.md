# Fundraising, investor research, and pitch narratives

Use for raising decisions, investor readiness, decks, fundraising models, updates, and diligence. Start from the company's goal, capital needs, milestones, business model, and jurisdiction. Venture funding is one option; compare revenue financing, bootstrapping, grants, debt, or other routes when relevant.

## Raise for a milestone

Define what the funding buys and what observable milestone it reaches. Model cash needs, operating plan, collection timing, downside, and buffer using [finance](finance.md). Distinguish planned fundraising from committed/received funds. Compare dilution, control, repayment, execution risk, and realistic timing without inventing market terms.

A simplified priced-round calculation can illustrate investment divided by post-money valuation, but label exclusions. SAFEs, notes, option pool changes, conversion order, pro-rata rights, and jurisdictions require actual terms and a proper cap-table model. Never present a simplified calculation as a finalized ownership outcome.

## Readiness and narrative

Evaluate the story against the intended investor type and stage: urgent problem, clear buyer, credible solution, evidence of demand/repeat value, acquisition, differentiated advantage, economics, team execution, and use of funds. Expose gaps rather than averaging them into a readiness score.

Write the requested narrative or actual deck. A flexible story is: problem and customer, why now, solution/demo, evidence, market from defensible inputs, alternatives and advantage, business model/distribution, team, milestones and ask. Use the available presentation workflow for a deck file; slide titles alone do not complete a deck request.

Traction charts need definitions, periods, actual data, and consistent revenue categories. Separate users, active users, customers, paid pilots, contracts, MRR, services, and cash. Do not fabricate testimonials, team credentials, customer logos, pipelines, investor interest, or market size. Make missing proof a visible editorial requirement, not a disguised fact.

## Investor targeting

Research current thesis, geography, stage, check size if published, recent relevant investments, and constraints from investor primary sources. Cite each material fit claim. A past investment is not proof a fund is currently investing. Distinguish fit from unknown availability, relationship, or likelihood of funding.

Prioritize a feasible list, explain fit and potential conflicts, and draft a concise relevant approach. Do not guess personal emails or imply warm introductions that do not exist. Sending outreach requires user authorization.

## Process and diligence

Build the materials needed for this raise: company story, model, cap table, product/metrics evidence, corporate documents, customer agreements, and relevant IP/compliance records. Use existing authorized folders and access controls. Do not publish confidential diligence merely to create a shareable link.

Track investor, owner/contact, source of fit, status, actual next step/date, questions, and evidence requested. Align the operating schedule with runway. An exploratory meeting, partner meeting, term sheet, signed commitment, and wired cash are different states.

For investor updates, write actual concise progress, metrics with comparables, challenges, learning, cash/runway context when appropriate, and specific asks. Preserve disappointing evidence. Publication or sending remains separate unless authorized.
