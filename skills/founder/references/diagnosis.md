# Diagnosis and founder decisions

Use for broad strategy, prioritization, pivots, board reviews, or a coherent business plan. Diagnose from observed behavior before treating a proposed fix as the cause. For a clearly scoped artifact, use its playbook directly.

## Locate the constraint

Map the relevant path: reachable customer → qualified attention → understood value → purchase/commitment → delivery/activation → repeat value → contribution → cash. Use counts, periods, and segment differences. Several problems can coexist; identify the intervention likely to change the outcome most within constraints.

| Observation | Competing explanations | Useful discriminator |
|---|---|---|
| Praise without commitment | Politeness, wrong buyer, low urgency, weak offer | Past behavior and a specific commitment |
| Qualified buyers never hear about it | Access, targeting, channel, timing | Trace buyers to actual communities or buying triggers |
| Visitors do not convert | Wrong traffic, unclear value, friction, mistrust, price | Source-specific funnel and buyer comprehension |
| Demos stall | Weak priority, no sponsor, procurement, insufficient proof | Buyer map and agreed next milestone |
| Users do not activate or return | Setup friction, wrong expectation, missing value | Core-job observation and retention cohorts |
| Sales grow while cash falls | Poor contribution, acquisition cost, inventory, collections | Unit economics and dated cash schedule |

Do not conclude pricing is the cause simply because a prospect says “too expensive.” Compare lack of value, budget, authority, and switching risk.

## Choose a move

Present the best-supported diagnosis, a plausible alternative, and an observation that distinguishes them. Prefer a reversible action under high uncertainty. Prioritize by consequence, evidence, reversibility, effort, cost, and time to useful evidence. If scores help compare options, expose assumptions and do not portray totals as probabilities.

A memo can cover: decision; why now; evidence; alternatives/tradeoffs; what must be true; completed next action; owner and revisit trigger. If evidence cannot support a verdict, specify a test instead of manufacturing a build/pivot/kill score.

## Board review

Apply distinct lenses such as urgency, differentiation/durability, product simplicity, distribution, and economics. Choose the lenses the decision needs. For each, give the strongest case, most damaging objection, missing evidence, and condition that would change the conclusion. Preserve meaningful disagreement rather than averaging away a fatal dependency.

These are analytic lenses, not endorsements or impersonations of named founders. Delegation is optional and subject to the environment's authorization; a simulated committee is not customer evidence.

## Pivot or continue

Compare actual results with the precommitted experiment, including whether it reached the intended buyer and whether execution was adequate. Decide whether to change segment, problem, offer, channel, delivery, or business model. Change the smallest causal element supported by evidence. One failed channel does not refute a problem; paid trials do not prove repeatable distribution.

For a business plan, synthesize relevant work into a consistent narrative and model: customer/problem evidence, offer, alternatives, acquisition, delivery/capacity, economics/cash, milestones, and assumptions. Separate “profitable under these inputs” from “validated business.” Produce the requested format without requiring every playbook first.
