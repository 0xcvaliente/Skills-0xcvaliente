# Founder-led sales and revenue operations

Use for first customers, prospecting, outreach drafts, discovery calls, demos, proposals, pipeline, and sales-to-delivery handoffs. Choose the sales motion from value, complexity, buying process, and founder capacity.

## Prospecting and outreach

Define the segment, role, trigger, relevant problem, current alternative, ability to buy, and exclusion criteria. For a prospect list, use verified public or authorized company sources. Separate buying signals from speculative intent. Do not fabricate contacts, scrape private accounts, or claim email delivery from guessed addresses.

Write messages grounded in a relevant observed event or role-level situation. Explain the plausible problem and specific useful outcome, with one low-friction ask. Personalization should connect to the job, not display unrelated personal knowledge. Never invent a relationship, shared customer, testimonial, or result.

For sequences, each follow-up should add relevant value or resolve an objection. Specify eligibility, delay, stop-on-reply/purchase/opt-out rules, and the intended response. Drafting is separate from sending. When sending is authorized, check recipient, exact content, and duplicates; verify outcome before retrying ambiguous sends.

## Discovery and demonstrations

Prepare known account facts, hypotheses, and questions. Explore the current process, problem frequency/consequence, previous attempts, priority, buyer authority, budget path, deadline, procurement, and criteria. Keep reported facts separate from seller assumptions.

Demonstrate the customer's job with credible data and an observable outcome. Avoid a feature tour unrelated to the buying decision. A useful call ends with an agreed milestone, owner, and date or an honest disqualification. “Send me information” is not a committed next step.

For call analysis, cite transcript passages or timestamps, distinguish participants, and extract pain, impact, objections, decision process, commitments, and missing evidence. Do not assign a numerical close probability without a calibrated basis.

## Offers, proposals, and deal plans

Write scope, outcome, deliverables, implementation responsibilities, timeline dependencies, price and cadence, acceptance, support, limits, and open terms. Check alignment with product capacity and contribution. Quantify ROI only from defensible buyer inputs with visible assumptions; do not promise a multiple because a pricing framework suggests it.

For complex B2B, map user, champion, economic buyer, technical reviewer, procurement, and blockers. Use a mutual action plan for validation, approval, contracting, and launch. A nonbinding expression of interest is not a booked contract; a booked contract is not collected cash.

## Pipeline and handoffs

Define stages by observable entry/exit criteria rather than arbitrary labels. Track segment/source, owner, buyer, problem, next action/date, value/currency, expected timing, and loss reason. Keep stages and definitions small enough to maintain.

Specify qualification, routing, response responsibility, duplicate handling, and sales-to-delivery acceptance before automating CRM workflows. Do not build a complex lead score without outcome data; explicit fit and readiness rules may work better initially.

Measure stage conversion, time in stage, no-next-action deals, win/loss by segment/source, realized contract value, collections, and post-sale outcomes as relevant. Use historical calibrated conversion for forecasting or show scenarios. Read [conversion and measurement](conversion-analytics.md) for attribution and intervention readback. A successful sales playbook requires delivery and retained value, not only more meetings.
