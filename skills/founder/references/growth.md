# Go-to-market, growth, and launch

Use for acquisition strategy, launch planning, distribution, channel selection, and marketing execution. Select the growth motion from buyer behavior, price, complexity, urgency, and founder capacity. Do not assume every business needs paid ads, outbound, PLG, or a public launch platform.

## Route to the actual work

- For content, social, email lifecycle, ad creative, repurposing, or editorial operations, read [content and creative](marketing-content.md).
- For CRO, signup/paywalls, tracking, A/B testing, attribution, or learning from campaign results, read [conversion and measurement](conversion-analytics.md).
- For SEO, AI visibility, competitor keywords, programmatic pages, or technical discovery, read [search and discovery](search-discovery.md).
- For prospecting, outreach, discovery, and deals, read [sales](sales.md).

Load only the reference relevant to the requested deliverable.

## Select a channel

Use observed customer presence and reachable access. If needed, read [market research](market-research.md). Compare time to signal, qualified volume, likely economics, channel rules, operational fit, and founder advantages. An existing audience or integration can help; follower counts and unqualified traffic do not establish demand.

Create a small, feasible channel test: segment and buying trigger, concrete distribution route, finished asset, buyer action, full funnel measurement, effort/spend cap, and continue/stop rule. Calculate reachable exposures × assumed conversion steps × realized revenue/contribution when forecasting; label assumptions and avoid promising results.

Evaluate qualified acquisition through activation, collected payments, retained value, and contribution. Cheap leads that never buy can be costly. A winning early channel may saturate or change buyer mix; include the observation that warrants a fresh channel search.

## Paid acquisition and growth loops

For ads, specify audience, offer, actual creative, landing destination, tracking, test budget, and unit-economics guardrails. Separate campaign design from launching it. Before an authorized launch, verify actual account, geography, budget, schedule, conversion event, and platform rules. Read back the live configuration; do not assume a successful API response proves the right ad is running.

For referrals, partnerships, communities, free tools, or embedded sharing, describe how a user gets value, what causes an invitation/recommendation, why the recipient cares, and the complete return path. Model cost and abuse. A share button alone is not a growth loop.

## Launch as a coordinated delivery event

Choose the audience and objective: paid pilots, first retained users, qualified pipeline, or a milestone announcement. Build the assets and operational plan the requested launch needs: page/listing, demo, offer, messages, support/owner coverage, instrumentation, and follow-up. Reuse existing work rather than generating a redundant asset library.

Check platform-specific dates, limits, and rules from current official sources. Rankings, votes, impressions, and community attention are intermediate signals. Do not promise a number-one launch or use fake engagement.

Tie the schedule to dependencies and available capacity. Include a live readiness check, failure/rollback action, and post-launch readback of qualified traffic, activation, revenue, and customer feedback. Prepare the launch fully before asking for missing publication authorization. Scheduling future checks requires the user to request recurring work; a plan may specify a review date without creating an automation.
