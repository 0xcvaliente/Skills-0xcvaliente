# Content, creative, and lifecycle marketing

Use when the task is a finished marketing asset or a repeatable content system. Begin with audience, buying stage, desired action, channel, actual offer, and available proof. Reuse company voice and known customer wording.

## Content that earns a business result

Choose topics from actual buyer questions, sales objections, support patterns, product usage, search intent, and source material. Map each piece to a useful job: discovery, evaluation, activation, renewal, or expansion. Popular topics unrelated to the buyer can inflate traffic without helping the business.

For a content brief, specify reader and situation, unique claim, source/proof, useful takeaway, structure, distribution route, CTA, and business outcome. For an asset request, write the asset itself. A calendar or list of hooks does not complete a request for a finished article or sequence.

Distinguish original evidence, cited external facts, and illustrative scenarios. Never invent case studies, quotations, screenshots, founder anecdotes, customer outcomes, or performance numbers. A chart should retain its input data, units, period, source, and transformation. Use an actual artifact workflow when the user requests a chart, image, deck, or video.

## Social and ad creative

Choose an angle grounded in the audience: concrete result, observed mistake, useful walkthrough, distinctive evidence, objection, or credible comparison. Match the platform and author's voice. Hooks must be paid off by the body. Avoid claiming virality or guaranteed conversion.

Produce meaningful variants that test an angle, offer, proof, or format. Do not generate dozens merely to raise a model score. Review for clarity, factual accuracy, audience fit, honest promise, and actual channel constraints. Model scoring is editorial judgment; user engagement, qualified response, and retained revenue are empirical outcomes.

For ads, keep creative, destination, and offer consistent. Verify current format limits and material claims. Include asset dimensions or copy constraints only when relevant to production; do not hardcode an old platform limit as timeless.

## Email and SMS lifecycle

Define the segment, trigger, eligibility/exclusions, goal, timing relative to behavior, and exit condition. Write subjects, actual bodies, CTA, and personalization fields. Coordinate with in-product events rather than sending a sequence regardless of activation, purchase, or cancellation.

Avoid sending a conversion sequence to existing purchasers or continuing after a reply/opt-out. Keep the consent and delivery requirements relevant to the chosen channel and market. Open rates can be distorted by privacy features; measure qualified actions and business outcomes. Use [sales](sales.md) for cold outreach.

## Repurposing and editorial operations

Extract source-backed ideas before adapting formats. Preserve context, qualifications, and speaker attribution. Repurposed pieces should work independently and suit the recipient channel; do not present unsupported paraphrases as verbatim quotes. For video, verify selected moments in the actual recording when visuals or timing affect the claim.

A useful production system records source, asset/version, audience, status, owner, publication destination, observed result, and lesson. Make the review proportional to factual and brand risk. Preserve the founder's editorial choices; a model's preferences must not become automatic publishing authority.

## Learn from the actual release

Attach the hypothesis and primary outcome to the exact asset/version. Compare the relevant cohort and time window, account for spend, list/source mix, and seasonality, and read back results before promoting a pattern. Retain failed experiments. A winning headline in one context is not a universal rule. Read [conversion and measurement](conversion-analytics.md) for causal tests and attribution.
