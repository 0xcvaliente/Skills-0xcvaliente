# Validation and customer learning

Use to test assumptions, design interviews, analyze responses, or plan a demand test. Start with the decision new evidence should change.

## State a falsifiable hypothesis

Define who, problem and trigger, current alternative, economic consequence, and proposed behavior. A broad assertion that managers will pay needs a specific buying event, offer, and measurable commitment.

Separate problem existence, buyer access, willingness to pay, delivery feasibility, activation, and repeat value. Choose the most consequential unresolved assumption. Do not build a polished product solely to test whether a problem exists.

## Interviews

Recruit from the intended segment, including non-buyers and people who chose another solution. Avoid relying solely on friends or an enthusiastic audience.

Ask about a specific recent occurrence: what happened, what they did, workarounds, time/money lost, decision-maker, actual spend, alternatives, and why it stayed unresolved. Explore buying triggers and switching anxiety. Ask permission to follow up or observe work. Pitch after learning past behavior; keep pitch reactions separate from problem evidence.

Produce the recruiting criteria, invitation draft, interview guide, and capture sheet when requested. Do not send invitations without authorization. In synthesis, show distinct respondents, contrary evidence, segment differences, traceable quotes, and recruitment limitations. A repeated theme is not measured market prevalence.

## Choose the lightest meaningful test

| Uncertainty | Test | What it can establish |
|---|---|---|
| Is the problem costly and recurrent? | Workflow observation and past-behavior interviews | Problem mechanism and current alternatives |
| Can the buyer be reached? | Targeted outreach or channel probe | Access and qualified response in that channel |
| Will they commit? | Priced pilot, paid concierge, transparent preorder | Specific buyer/offer commitment |
| Can value be delivered? | Manual or constrained prototype | Delivery feasibility and time to value |
| Is it worth repeating or renewing? | Usage and renewal cohorts | Repeat value over the relevant interval |

Specify audience, actual intervention, exposure denominator, primary outcome, quality/harm guardrails, proposed decision threshold and rationale, collection method, time/cost cap, and action on pass/fail/inconclusive. Set thresholds before results, based on economics and the next commitment. A handful of responses does not establish statistical validation.

A waitlist measures that signup offer. A nonbinding letter is weaker than payment; signed commitments still need terms checked. Deposits, preorders, and pilots must make delivery/refund expectations explicit.

## Simulated customer critique

When requested, use plausible situations to surface objections and compare comprehension. Ground personas in research and label invented details. Report objections, unsupported premises, and real-world tests. Do not calculate synthetic purchase probabilities, derive demand curves from generated respondents, or feed simulated buying answers into revenue forecasts. More generated personas do not create independent market evidence.
