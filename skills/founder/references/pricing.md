# Pricing, packaging, and offers

Use for initial prices, tier design, business models, offers, paywalls, discounts, or pricing changes. Price is part of the product and delivery model, not a final cosmetic choice.

## Triangulate a feasible offer

Connect customer value and alternatives, buyer budget/procurement, delivery cost and capacity, acquisition cost, and observed willingness to commit. Competitor prices anchor comparison but do not establish your demand curve. Distinguish desired outcomes from what the buyer can realistically attribute to the product.

Evaluate a value metric by how it tracks benefit, buyer predictability, measurement, abuse, and cost. Seats can penalize collaboration; usage pricing can create bill anxiety; outcomes require agreed measurement; fixed pricing can expose cost risk. For AI products, include inference/retries, heavy usage, human escalation, and changing provider costs.

Design the few packages that correspond to genuinely distinct needs. Keep required value in a usable package. Explain entitlements, limits, overage, support, billing cadence, cancellation, and migration. Do not force three tiers or a free plan where the economics or segment does not justify them.

Run contribution and cash calculations using [finance](finance.md). A cost floor is not proof buyers will pay; a high willingness to pay is not proof you can deliver profitably. Separate recurring revenue, setup fees, services, and one-time transactions.

## Offer design

Reduce the buyer's relevant friction: time to value, setup effort, proof, uncertainty, migration, or delivery risk. Bonuses should support the outcome and have a known cost. Guarantees need eligibility, remedy, measurement, time window, and funded fulfillment. Use scarcity or urgency only when a real constraint or deadline exists.

Compare free trials, paid pilots, freemium, entry products, and annual prepayment by conversion quality, variable costs, support, cash timing, and product readiness. Annual cash receipts are not twelve months of MRR growth, and prepaid service obligations remain real.

## Validate before committing broadly

Use actual purchase or pilot decisions when available. Ask about current spend, budget, approval path, tradeoffs, and the consequence of alternatives. Price surveys can inform exploration, but stated willingness is not conversion. If analyzing a real price-sensitivity survey, check respondent fit, sample selection, answer consistency, and method limits. Do not generate fake respondents for pricing analysis.

Propose an experiment with segment, offer, price/cadence, exposure, primary outcome, contribution/refund/support guardrails, collection method, cap, and decision rule. Test enough to change the decision; avoid implying precision unsupported by the sample. Read [conversion and measurement](conversion-analytics.md) for a randomized test.

## Changing existing prices

Model new versus existing customers, contract commitments, renewal dates, grandfathering, discount leakage, collection, and customer communication. Evaluate realized revenue and retention, not only checkout conversion. Prepare actual pricing copy, account-impact calculations, and announcement drafts when requested. Change live billing or send notices only within authorized scope.

Deliver a recommendation with package details, assumptions, economics, tradeoffs, and a test or rollout decision. Distinguish a proposed price from a validated price.
