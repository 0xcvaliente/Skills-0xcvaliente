# Positioning, brand, and copy

Use for positioning choices, messaging, naming, landing-page copy, or a pitch narrative. Build from known buyer behavior, current alternatives, differentiation the product can deliver, and permitted proof.

## Positioning decisions

Identify the customer and buying event, what they would use without this product, capabilities that matter, their resulting outcome, and the category that makes comparison understandable. “AI-powered” is a mechanism; translate it into a customer benefit with evidence and tradeoffs.

Choose a beachhead when shared needs, access, and delivery make it useful. Do not infer that a small niche is always the right long-term market. Compare an established category with a new framing by buyer comprehension, differentiation, search/distribution, and education cost.

Write a positioning brief: target and poor-fit segment; buying trigger; alternatives; meaningful advantage; proof; promise; objections; category. A brand promise cannot outrun current product capability. If claims depend on planned features, label those and exclude them from launch copy.

## Finished copy

Use a sequence suited to the page and buyer: recognizable problem/outcome, understandable product, concrete proof or demonstration, how it works, key objections, and a clear next step. Features should connect to benefits and proof. Above the fold, make the intended user, value, and action understandable.

Write actual headlines, body sections, CTA labels, microcopy, and relevant metadata when requested. Give alternatives only when they expose a meaningful messaging choice. Match the user's voice and channel; do not imitate a public figure or force every paragraph into a named persuasion formula.

Use real customer wording where supplied. Trace statistics, testimonials, logos, savings, certifications, and comparisons. If proof is missing, use honest demonstrations or a clearly marked editorial slot for future evidence. Never make an editorial placeholder look like a publishable testimonial. For ROI claims, show the measured baseline, interval, calculation, and limits.

Handle objections according to cause: trust needs credible proof, complexity needs explanation or assistance, switching risk needs migration evidence, price needs economic clarity. Counterfeit urgency and unsupported guarantees undermine the offer.

For content programs and lifecycle sequences, read [content and creative](marketing-content.md). For a page audit or test, read [conversion and measurement](conversion-analytics.md).

## Naming and visual briefs

Generate names against explicit criteria: audience comprehension, memorability, pronunciation, category fit, geographic/language conflicts, and extensibility. Separate creative preference from checked domain/handle availability. A public search is not trademark clearance; research current registries when legal availability is material.

Create a visual brief tied to audience, category expectations, proof, and channel constraints. Use an available design workflow for an actual visual deliverable; prose describing a page does not complete a request to build one.

## Quality review

Check whether a buyer can identify the product, benefit, fit, and next step; whether each material claim is supported; and whether copy aligns with actual pricing and onboarding. Simulated critique can improve clarity but cannot predict conversion lift. If a requested review uses scoring, report concrete defects and revisions alongside the subjective scores.
