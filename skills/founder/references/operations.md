# Operations, hiring, and execution

Use for operating cadence, SOPs, capacity, suppliers, hiring, team responsibilities, and execution risk. Adapt to the business; avoid adding enterprise process to a small founder team.

## Make a process executable

For an SOP, specify purpose, trigger, owner, required inputs/access, actions, decision points, output, acceptance checks, exception/recovery path, and escalation. Include the details someone needs to run it rather than generic “ensure quality” instructions. Verify a realistic example or walkthrough when practical.

Use observed current work, then simplify. Remove steps only after understanding their purpose. A process can contain accounting, safety, contractual, or customer obligations that are invisible in its happy path. Automation should follow a coherent process rather than replicate broken handoffs faster.

For delivery/capacity, map workload, lead time, dependencies, bottleneck, quality, staffing, and demand variability. Tie sales promises to actual fulfillment. For physical businesses, include suppliers, stock, spoilage/returns, equipment, location, opening schedules, and relevant local approvals. For services, include scope creep, utilization, collections, and client handoff.

## Hiring and delegation

Define the outcome a role must own, current work burden, needed capabilities, success measures, interface with the team, budget, and alternatives such as process improvement or contractors. Do not recommend a hire solely from a generic company stage.

Produce a role scorecard, job description, interview/work-sample plan, or onboarding process when requested. Assess actual evidence against job-relevant criteria. Do not infer protected traits or substitute a synthetic panel score for evidence of performance.

Use delegation when requested or authorized by the environment, with a clear brief, deliverable, access boundaries, and review responsibility. A task plan does not grant authority to message employees or change their assignments in external systems.

## Operating cadence

Choose a manageable objective, leading indicator, outcome metric, owner, next action, and decision date. A weekly review can cover results versus expectation, bottleneck, customer learning, cash/capacity, and the next experiment. Frequency should fit the work; a slow purchasing cycle may need a different interval.

Extract commitments from meeting notes with owner, action, due date, dependencies, and uncertainty. Do not invent agreement from a discussion or assign an absent person a task as if they consented.

## Risk and commitments

Prioritize risks by plausible impact and evidence, including concentration, supplier/platform dependency, collection, quality, key-person gaps, and regulatory obligations relevant to the business. Give owner, mitigation, observable trigger, and contingency. Keep subjective likelihood clearly labeled.

Research current local rules when permits, employment, taxes, or regulated claims matter. Do not generalize a rule from another country or produce a blanket compliance exercise for an unrelated SOP.

When recommending recurring monitoring or automation, describe the trigger, meaningful change, output, owner, and stop condition. Create a recurring task only when the user asks for recurring work.
